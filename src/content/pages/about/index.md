## Mission

### Art | Conservation | Education

Wahoo Bay is a public marine education park at the Hillsboro Inlet, inspiring ocean literacy through hands-on learning, immersive field experiences, and community conservation.

#### Art

Creation of unique, transformative and living public art to raise awareness and inspire emotional connections.

#### Conservation

Deploy host structures that facilitate the natural colonization of marine habitats to protect and conserve our natural marine ecosystem.

#### Education

To create public awareness about the plight of the marine environment by inspiring support of conservation efforts of our shores.

### Living Laboratory, Lasting Change

Where curiosity meets conservation, and every visitor makes an impact.

### Our Mission In Action

At Wahoo Bay, we are building a living laboratory where education, research, and conservation come together. Our mission is to connect people of all ages with the ocean through hands-on learning, innovative science, and community partnerships. Every program we offer is designed to spark curiosity, inspire stewardship, and create lasting impact for future generations.

### Anchored In Community

Wahoo Bay was created by and for the community — a place where families, students, educators, and ocean advocates can learn and grow together. From field trips and volunteer programs to conservation projects, we work alongside partners and neighbors to protect our local waters. Together, we are making the ocean accessible, engaging, and inspiring for all.

### From Sea to Shore, reef to classroom

Every wave tells a story, and at Wahoo Bay, we're writing a new chapter for our coastlines and communities. What started as a vision to bring the reef to the shoreline has grown into a hub for exploration, discovery, and action. Here, curiosity meets conservation, and every visitor has the chance to be part of something bigger — protecting the ocean we all depend on.

## Turning Passion into Impact

What began as a vision to bring the reef closer to the community has grown into a full-scale effort blending education, conservation, and collaboration. From the early ideas of Shipwreck Park to the first reef installations, and now the creation of Wahoo Bay as a living marine laboratory, each milestone reflects the passion of people determined to make the ocean accessible to all. This timeline highlights the key moments and partners who turned an ambitious dream into a lasting impact.

### Our Journey

#### 2025: Living Laboratory & Growing Community Engagement

![Volunteer divers surveying the reef at Wahoo Bay](/images/about/2025-gd-wb-816-lwb-06.jpg)

*"A shoreline reef for all, where curiosity meets conservation."*

Wahoo Bay continues to grow as a shoreline reef and immersive marine park, connecting students, families, and communities to ocean conservation. Momentum builds as the community dives in. Volunteer programs and partnerships expand, connecting local schools, divers, and residents with ocean conservation through hands-on activities.

#### 2024: Partnerships and Public Access

![Students and instructors collecting water samples on the Wahoo Bay dock](/images/about/2024-pxl_20250611_144923455.mp.jpg)

*"A living classroom powered by data, discovery, and community collaboration."*

Partnership with FAU Lab Schools and Ocean First Institute expands programs. Monthly field trips begin. Park opens daily to visitors. Data portal evolves into a K–12 engagement platform.

#### 2023: Education & Engagement Begin

![Seahive installation for coral restoration](/images/about/2023-seahive-installation.jpg)

*"From reef to classroom — the first students step into Wahoo Bay."*

First SEAHIVES deployed, first school field trip hosted, and WahooBay.net portal development begins showcasing real-time data and curriculum.

#### 2022: Project Greenlight

![Underwater camera documentation of marine life](/images/about/2022-jimmy-underwater-camera-2.jpg)

*"Partnerships, permits, and purpose align — Wahoo Bay is ready to rise."*

Permits finalized, weather station installed with FAU I-SENSE, and SEAHIVES fabrication begins. Water quality monitoring system added.

#### 2021: First Installation

![Wahoo Bay swim platform installed](/images/about/2021-wahoobay-swim-platform-installed.jpeg)

*"Green lights and first funding — momentum begins to build."*

ACOE and County permits secured for swim platform and SEAHIVES. Florida Fish & Wildlife provides $50,000 in support.

#### 2020: Permits and Studies

![Location of Wahoo Bay facility](/images/about/2020-location-of-wahoo-bay.jpg)

*"From concept to concrete — building the foundation for Wahoo Bay."*

City secures title to submerged lands. Permit applications filed and wave/current studies confirm safe swim boundaries.

#### 2019: Protecting the Waters

![Hillsboro inlet historical view](/images/about/2019-hillsboro-inlet-1947.jpg)

*"Safeguarding the inlet to preserve its future."*

Seagrass survey updated and fishing restrictions enacted to protect inlet waters.

#### 2017: Early Steps & Reef Expansion

<!-- katie: review: new description of this photo; the old site's ("Okinawa marine research activities") did not match it. -->
![A mermaid sculpture on the deck of the Okinawa shipwreck, divers behind it](/images/about/2017-okinawa-mermaid.jpg)

*"Not everyone can dive a shipwreck — so we dreamed of bringing the reef to shore."*

Initial seagrass surveys conducted and first planning meetings held with state and federal agencies. The Okinawa shipwreck is sunk, expanding Shipwreck Park's artificial reef system.

#### 2016: Shipwreck Park Launches

![Lady Luck vessel before artificial reef deployment](/images/about/2016-lady-luck-1a.jpg)

*"A shipwreck becomes a reef, and a vision takes root."*

The Lady Luck shipwreck is sunk off Pompano Beach, creating one of Florida's largest artificial reefs and sparking a wave of community-driven ocean conservation efforts.

## Meet the Wahoo Bay Team

Leaders in Conservation, Education & Community Impact

Our board and staff unite leaders from public service, academia, research, and design—combining decades of expertise in environmental education, engineering, and community development. Together, they advance Wahoo Bay's mission to inspire conservation through art, science, and collaboration.

### Leadership Across Industries

From casino and real estate development to municipal management and city planning, our team includes executives who have guided multimillion-dollar projects and revitalization efforts across Pompano Beach and Broward County.

### Scientific & Academic Excellence

Our members include university professors, PhD researchers, and program directors from Florida Atlantic University, Keiser University, and Everglades University—advancing research in marine ecology, engineering, and sustainable design.

### Public Service & Fiscal Expertise

With over 30 years in government leadership, team members have overseen capital improvement projects, managed city budgets exceeding $180 million, and established policies that shape resilient coastal communities.

### Education & Mentorship

Nationally recognized educators and research coordinators have spent decades mentoring students and teachers, developing STEM curricula, and fostering access to hands-on environmental learning.

### Community Engagement & Storytelling

Through public outreach, digital media, and volunteer networks, Wahoo Bay's team connects residents, partners, and visitors to the beauty and importance of South Florida's marine environment.
