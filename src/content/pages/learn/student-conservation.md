## The Flat Tree Oyster Search

Working with Florida Gulf Coast University researchers, volunteers survey hard substrates — seawalls, oyster reef, mangrove roots — for the flat tree oyster *Isognomon alatus* and its neighbors: oysters, mussels, barnacles, and hydroids.

No lab experience needed. A [standard protocol](/docs/oyster-search-sop.pdf), a datasheet, and 30 minutes of searching turn a shoreline walk into real distribution data.

![SEAHIVE structures being installed at Wahoo Bay](/images/about/2023-seahive-installation.jpg)

## How a survey works

1. **Pick a site:** A discrete search area roughly 100 m across, covering intertidal and shallow hard substrate — seawall, shell piles, mangrove roots.
2. **Search 30 minutes:** Record the sessile (attached) species you find — the survey covers oysters, mussels, barnacles and lacy hydroids, not mobile animals.
3. **Score abundance:** Rate each species' cover with simple Braun-Blanquet scores from 0.1 (very rare) to 5 (over 75% cover) using a visual key.
4. **Photograph & submit:** Photos plus the one-page datasheet (site, coordinates, substrate, scores) go to the FGCU research team.

## What you're looking for

Seven groups of shoreline animals, told apart by shell shape and how they attach. Full photo key in the ID guide.

<!-- katie: review: the accepted spelling of the scorched mussel's genus is Brachidontes; this list and the FGCU datasheet both say "Brachiodontes". Fix both or neither. -->
- **Flat Tree Oyster** (*Isognomon alatus*): The target species — flat, fan-shaped shells attached by byssal threads.
- **Eastern Oyster** (*Crassostrea virginica*): Cemented on; rough, irregular shell shapes.
- **Green Mussel** (*Perna viridis*): Smooth, vivid jade color; byssal attachment.
- **Hooked Mussel** (*Ischadium recurvum*): Very curved with pronounced grooves; byssal attachment.
- **Scorched Mussel** (*Brachiodontes exustus*): Smaller, lighter (often yellowish), finer grooves; byssal attachment.
- **Barnacles** (*Cirripedia*): Cemented; teepee-shaped exoskeleton with "doors" at the top.
- **Lacy Hydroids** (*Leptothecata*): Pale yellow/brown wiry branching structure with tiny polyps.

## Research, workshops & mentorship

Wahoo Bay is a living laboratory: SEAHIVE shoreline structures, live environmental sensors, an underwater camera, and a public data dashboard — all available as a platform for university research and coursework through three partner programs.

### In-Situ Research & Technology Testing

Use Wahoo Bay as a field-testing site for marine sensors, water-quality monitoring technologies, autonomous underwater vehicles, habitat mapping, AI and computer-vision species identification, artificial reef components, and environmental DNA sampling. Wahoo Bay provides site access and coordination, equipment deployment assistance, and integration with its long-term monitoring datasets — from short-term testing agreements to multi-year deployments.

### Fundamentals of Research Workshop Series

Professional development for 9th–12th graders, undergraduates, graduate students and early-career researchers: Research Design, Research Ethics, Scientific Communication, Grant Writing, Career Preparedness, and Scientific Publishing. Offered as individual workshops, multi-week research training programs, or summer research academies.

### Student Research Internship & Mentorship Program (SRIMP)

Faculty bring an existing research project and a student cohort to Wahoo Bay; the faculty member serves as Principal Investigator while Wahoo Bay staff provide co-mentorship, field expertise, and project coordination. Engage through semester cohorts, summer programs, graduate thesis projects, or multi-year collaborations.
